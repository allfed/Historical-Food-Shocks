ALLFED · Historical Food Shocks

A Global Catastrophic Food Failure isn’t hypothetical — it happens locally, somewhere, almost every year.

A Global Catastrophic Food Failure (GCFF) is a collapse in food production severe enough to threaten global food security. Nobody has seen one at global scale — but this paper finds that shocks of that same size and speed have repeatedly hit individual countries since 1961. This page lets you see them directly: where they happened, how severe they were, and what caused them.

That collapse wasn’t a one-off. This has been the pattern for six decades:

Six decades of shocks, decade by decade

Each map shows how much of that decade a country spent in shock — production more than 5% below trend, the same threshold used throughout the paper. No decade, and almost no region, is spared. If an event of this size and cause hit global food production all at once instead of one country at a time, that is what a Global Catastrophic Food Failure would look like.

What’s behind these shocks?

Each event was categorized by reviewing historical reporting for that country and year (with an LLM-assisted first pass, verified by hand). Climate shocks — mostly drought — dominate, but conflict, economic collapse, and policy failure are all recurring causes. Click a category to highlight it on the map above.
